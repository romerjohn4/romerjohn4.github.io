Case study / Web application

JAMPER Finance.

A team-built personal finance web application, from requirements and interface design to development and demonstration.

RoleLead UI/UX Designer & Full-Stack Developer
Team6 members
TimelineSept 2024 – Apr 2025

01 / Project overview

One place for financial clarity

JAMPER Finance was a team capstone project developed at SAIT. The goal was to bring common personal finance tasks—such as reviewing transactions, budgets, savings goals, and debts—into a unified web experience.

My role combined leading the interface design with contributing to implementation. I created early paper sketches, high-fidelity screens, and an interactive Figma prototype, and collaborated with five teammates as the feature scope evolved.

02 / Early exploration

Starting with paper, not pixels

Before moving into Figma, I sketched early layouts to explore how the application could organize financial information. The first dashboard sketch used a horizontal navigation menu, followed by an overview of metrics, transaction records, and budget insights.

Additional sketches explored savings goals, including progress and an add-goal form, and a debt tracking page with loan details and payment actions. These were working ideas, not final specifications; the interface changed as our team refined the scope.

03 / Iteration & design decisions

Why the navigation changed

My original dashboard concept placed navigation across the top. As the team agreed on more features, the horizontal menu became increasingly crowded. I considered a dropdown menu, but it would add development complexity and make key destinations less immediately visible. I chose a persistent left sidebar instead, giving each section a clear place while preserving space for charts and content.

Design evolution: horizontal menu in the paper sketch → persistent sidebar in the high-fidelity designs.

Choosing a calmer visual language

I proposed soft light green, white surfaces, and darker green accents to evoke finance without making the application feel visually heavy. A teammate suggested a darker neon-green direction; after reviewing both, the team voted for the lighter palette.

These choices were informed by our team's design discussions. We did not conduct formal usability testing with external users, so I would validate the navigation and visual hierarchy with users in a future iteration.

04 / High-fidelity designs

Turning concepts into screens

In Figma, I translated the evolving layouts into more detailed interfaces and an interactive prototype. The dashboard focuses on balance history, recent transactions, and weekly activity. Supporting screens show ways to set savings goals and review debts.

05 / Delivery & reflection

Designed with a team, built under real constraints

Feedback during development came from my teammates, rather than a formal end-user usability study. We iterated on screens as requirements changed and worked toward a completed capstone demonstration at SAIT CapCon.

This case study shows design artifacts from the project. Some application features remained technically incomplete at the end of the capstone; the screens shown here are high-fidelity designs, not evidence of fully working backend functionality.

What I'd improve next

With more time, I'd conduct usability tests with target users, check accessibility and responsive behavior across more devices, and refine the empty states and financial data explanations.
